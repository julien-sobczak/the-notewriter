## BookReview: My Review

@read_date: 2025-08-12

In this essay, Seneca tackles one of philosophy's most fundamental questions: what constitutes a happy life? His answer, rooted in Stoic principles, emphasizes virtue over pleasure and inner peace over external circumstances.

Seneca doesn't promise easy happiness or quick fixes. Instead, he argues that true happiness comes from living according to reason and nature, from mastering oneself rather than accumulating possessions or pursuing pleasures.

What makes this work valuable is its balance. Seneca doesn't demand asceticism or reject all pleasure. He simply argues that happiness must be found within, not in the fleeting and unreliable external world. A thoughtful guide for anyone seeking lasting contentment.
