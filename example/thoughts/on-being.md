# On Being

## Note: The Authentic Self

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentic_leadership`

> "To be yourself in a world that is constantly trying to make you something else is the greatest accomplishment."
> -- Ralph Waldo Emerson

In today's hyperconnected world, authenticity means resisting the pressure to curate a perfect online persona. It's about being comfortable with your values and choices, even when they don't align with trending opinions or popular lifestyles. Your authentic self emerges when you stop performing for others and start living according to your own principles.

## Note: Present Moment Awareness

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness`

> "The present moment is the only time over which we have dominion."
> -- Thích Nhất Hạnh

Modern life bombards us with notifications, deadlines, and endless to-do lists that pull our attention into the future or past. Being present means putting down your phone during meals, fully listening when someone speaks, and noticing the sensations of your daily activities. It's the antidote to the anxiety that comes from always planning the next thing.

## Note: Acceptance of Imperfection

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-compassion`

> "Have no fear of perfection - you'll never reach it."
> -- Salvador Dalí

In an era of Instagram filters and LinkedIn highlight reels, we forget that everyone struggles. Being means accepting your flaws as part of your humanity, not obstacles to fix. It's recognizing that done is better than perfect, and that your worth isn't determined by your productivity or achievements.

## Note: Inner Peace

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_peace`

> "Peace comes from within. Do not seek it without."
> -- Buddha

We often think the next promotion, relationship, or purchase will bring us peace. But being at peace means finding contentment in the present, regardless of external circumstances. It's the practice of gratitude, meditation, and self-reflection that helps us realize we already have everything we need.

## Note: Living with Purpose

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikigai`

> "The purpose of life is not to be happy. It is to be useful, to be honorable, to be compassionate, to have it make some difference that you have lived and lived well."
> -- Ralph Waldo Emerson

Being purposeful doesn't require grand gestures or life-changing careers. It's about finding meaning in small acts: mentoring a colleague, being reliable for your family, or contributing to your community. Your purpose emerges from the intersection of what you're good at, what you enjoy, and what the world needs.

## Note: Emotional Intelligence

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_intelligence`

> "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response."
> -- Viktor Frankl

Being emotionally intelligent means pausing before reacting to that frustrating email or difficult conversation. It's about recognizing your emotions without being controlled by them, and understanding that others' actions often reflect their struggles, not your worth. This space between stimulus and response is where wisdom lives.

## Note: Self-Compassion

`@source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-compassion`

> "Talk to yourself like you would to someone you love."
> -- Brené Brown

We often speak to ourselves more harshly than we would to our worst enemy. Being compassionate with yourself means treating your mistakes as learning opportunities, not character flaws. When you fail or struggle, ask yourself: "What would I say to a friend in this situation?" Then offer yourself that same kindness.
